First-Time Buyer in Kent: After You Get the Keys

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | November 2026

When your solicitor confirms completion, the keys are released, usually by the estate agent. Then the order is simple: check the property and take photos and meter readings, move in, sort a bed and the basics for the first night, and deal with insurance, council tax, address changes and broadband in the first week. Everything else can wait for the first month. This guide follows that sequence, with the practical points a removals crew sees on the day.

When you actually get the keys on completion day

Keys are released once the solicitors confirm that the money has arrived. That depends on banks, solicitors and often other people in a chain, so the time varies. It is rarely first thing in the morning. Many buyers get keys in the early afternoon, and some later than that. Treat any time you are given as an estimate, not a promise.

If you are in a chain, your completion can only happen once the buyer above or below you has also had their money sent through. One late payment can hold everyone up. While you wait, stay reachable, keep your phone charged and do not start loading a van on the assumption that you will be in by lunchtime.

Plan for a late start. Keep the essentials with you rather than packed in the van, and tell your removal company early if the key release looks like it will slip. A crew that knows at 10am can often adapt. A crew that finds out at 3pm has fewer options.

If you are unsure what is happening, call in this order:

  1. Your solicitor, who can see whether the funds have arrived.
  2. The estate agent, who releases the keys.
  3. Your removal company, so they can adjust the arrival time.

The first hour in your new home: checks before you unpack

It is tempting to start carrying boxes in straight away. Spend the first hour on checks while the rooms are still empty, because that is when problems are easiest to see and to prove.

Walk through with the paperwork

Take the sale details and any list of agreed fixtures and fittings and go room by room. Check that what was included is still there, such as light fittings, curtain poles, kitchen appliances or a shed. Check for anything left behind that should not be.

Photograph every room

Take dated photos of each room, including floors, walls, ceilings, windows and the garden. Do this before furniture goes in. If you later find damage or something missing, you will have a record to give your solicitor.

Meter readings and key locations

  • Take photos of the gas, electric and water meter readings, and note where each meter is.
  • Find the water stopcock, the consumer unit (fuse box) and the boiler. If a pipe bursts or a circuit trips, you will want to know where these are without searching.
  • Turn on the heating and run the hot water to check both work.

Doors, windows and locks

Test every door, window and lock, and count the keys you have been given. If you are unsure who else might hold spare keys, plan to change the locks. It is a common and sensible precaution.

Left-behind items and problems

If something is missing, damaged or has been left that should not be there, tell your solicitor promptly and send your photos. Do not wait until the end of the month.

Moving day for first-time buyers: keep it simple

First-time buyers often have less furniture than people moving up the ladder, so the main question is what size of job it is. A small amount of furniture and a handful of boxes may suit a man and van. A full flat or house of furniture usually needs a proper crew and a larger van. Volume and access decide it, not just the number of bedrooms. Our house removals in Kent page explains how we approach full moves.

Check access before the day

Look at parking, stairs and lift access at the new address, and tell the removal company in advance. A long carry from the van, a narrow stairwell, a third-floor flat or a permit-only street all affect how the move runs. If you can, ask the sellers or the agent about parking rules before completion day.

Keep these with you, not in the van

  • Keys, ID and your completion paperwork
  • Phone and chargers
  • Any medication
  • A kettle, mugs, tea and coffee
  • A few basic tools, such as a screwdriver and a tape measure

Label boxes by room

Our crews place items in the correct rooms, but they can only do that if the boxes say where they go. Write the room on the side of each box, not just the top, because boxes get stacked.

The first night: what to sort before bed

You do not need to unpack the house on day one. You need a bed, a way to make a hot drink and a quick safety check.

  • Make the bed first. Pack one box with bedding, towels and toiletries and put it where you can find it.
  • Find the basics: kettle, a few plates, cutlery, tea and coffee.
  • Check safety items: look for smoke alarms and a carbon monoxide alarm, and make sure you know how to switch off the power and water.
  • Leave heavy unpacking until tomorrow. Tired people unpack badly and lose things.

The first week: admin that cannot wait

These jobs are time sensitive, so do them in the first few days if you can.

  • Insurance. Buildings and contents cover should be in place. Buildings cover normally needs to start from exchange of contracts, not completion, so check with your solicitor and lender that this is already done.
  • Council tax. Tell your local council about the move. In Medway, that is Medway Council. Elsewhere in Kent, it is the relevant district council.
  • Utilities. Register with your gas, electricity and water suppliers and submit the meter readings you photographed.
  • Broadband. Order it early. Installs can take time, and you may need it for work.
  • Change your address. Your bank, employer, GP, DVLA and others all need to know. Our list of who to tell about your new address covers the full set.
  • Post. Redirect your mail if you want it, particularly while accounts catch up.

The first month: the less obvious jobs

  • Keep your completion paperwork safe. Your solicitor normally handles Land Registry registration of your ownership. Ask them to confirm when it has been done.
  • Learn your bin days. Collection days and recycling rules differ between areas, so check your council's website.
  • List repairs and projects. Rank them by safety first, then cost, then how urgent they are. Not everything needs doing now.
  • Consider storage if you have too much. If furniture does not fit yet, or you are waiting on a project, secure short and long term storage can give you breathing space while you settle.
  • Keep a small buffer fund. First repairs tend to surface within weeks, such as a dripping tap, a stiff lock or a fault with an appliance.

Common first-time buyer mistakes after getting the keys

  • Not photographing the property before moving in. Without photos, it is hard to show what condition you found it in.
  • Leaving insurance and utilities until after moving day. Sort them beforehand where you can.
  • Booking removals too late or without checking access. Late bookings limit your choice of dates, and surprises with parking or stairs slow the day down.
  • Throwing out packing materials or paperwork too soon. Keep boxes and paper until you are sure everything is accounted for.
  • Trying to unpack everything in one weekend. Do bedrooms, the kitchen and the bathroom first. The rest can take weeks.

Frequently asked questions

What time do I get the keys on completion day?

There is no fixed time. Keys are released after your solicitor confirms the money has arrived, and this is rarely first thing in the morning. A chain can push it later. Ask your solicitor and the estate agent for an expected time, and treat it as an estimate.

What should I do first when I get the keys to my first home?

Walk through with your sale paperwork, photograph every room, take meter readings and find the stopcock, consumer unit and boiler. Then test the heating, hot water, doors and windows before you start unpacking.

Do I need to change the locks when I move into a new house?

It is not a legal requirement, but many people do it if they are not sure who holds spare keys. It is a sensible precaution, particularly if you were not handed every key or if you have any doubts.

What should I do if the previous owner left rubbish or items behind?

Photograph it and contact your solicitor promptly. Whether the items were meant to stay depends on what was agreed in the sale. Do not dispose of anything of value until you have checked.
