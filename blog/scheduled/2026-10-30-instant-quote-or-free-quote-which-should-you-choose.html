Instant Quote or Free Quote: Which Should You Choose?

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | October 2026

When you are planning a move in Kent, there are now two ways to get a price from Medway and Kent Removals. You can use the instant quote and pay tool and get a fixed price online in a couple of minutes, or you can ask for a free quote and talk it through with us. Both lead to a fixed price. They suit different kinds of move, and different kinds of people. This guide helps you choose.

What the instant quote does

The instant quote tool asks for a few simple details: the size of your property, the postcode you are moving from, the postcode you are moving to, and the date you want. It works out the drive time between the two postcodes, and then builds a fixed price from the size of the job, the number of vans and the length of the journey. The price appears on screen as you make your choices, so you can see how each change affects it.

If you are happy, you can book there and then. You can pay a 10% deposit, pay in full by card, or for smaller moves pay with Klarna. You receive a confirmation by email straight away. You do not need to wait for a call, a visit or an email reply.

What the free quote does

A free quote starts with a conversation. You tell us about your move through the contact form or by phone, and we ask questions that a form cannot. How much furniture is there? Is there parking outside? Are there stairs, a loft, a garage, a piano or a garden shed full of things? We then give you a fixed price based on what we have heard, and we respond to every enquiry within 60 minutes, seven days a week.

When the instant quote is the right choice

The instant quote works best when your move is fairly standard.

  • A typical home: a studio, flat or house that fits one of the standard sizes, with a normal amount of furniture and belongings.
  • Straightforward access: reasonable parking and no unusual obstacles at either end.
  • A firm date in mind: you know when you want to move and want to secure it.
  • You value speed: you would rather settle the price now than wait for a callback.
  • You like to compare on your own time: evenings and weekends suit you better than office hours.

Many people also use it simply to see a realistic price before deciding anything. Because the tool starts empty and the price builds as you choose, it is a low-pressure way to see what a move would cost.

When a free quote is the better first step

Sometimes a conversation saves you money and trouble.

  • Unusual items: a piano, a safe, a very large wardrobe, or artwork that needs special care.
  • Difficult access: narrow stairs, long carries from the van, or restricted parking that may need a permit.
  • A very full property: if you are not sure whether your belongings fit the size you have chosen.
  • Long distances: for journeys of five hours or more, we confirm a fixed price with you directly.
  • Packing and extras: packing is a separate service, booked separately after a survey, so it is easier to discuss it by phone.
  • Complicated timing: if your date depends on a chain and you need to talk through the options.

There is no cost or obligation either way. A free quote is not a lesser route, only a more conversational one.

What the price covers

Whichever route you take, the quoted price is a fixed price for the move you have described, with no VAT added. It includes the crew and vehicles for the job, furniture blankets and protection, dismantling and reassembly of standard beds and flat pack furniture, goods in transit insurance and public liability insurance, and placement of your items in the correct rooms at the other end. If you want to understand how the figures are put together, our guide to how the instant removals quote works goes through it step by step.

Could the two prices differ?

They can, and that is not a sign of a mistake. The instant quote is built from the standard size and distance you enter. A free quote is built from the detail you give us in conversation. If your move is standard, the two should land in the same place. If your move has unusual features, the conversation may add detail that the form could not see, and the price reflects it. Either way, the price you agree is the price you pay.

Can you use both?

Yes, and plenty of people do. A good approach is to run the instant quote first for a realistic figure, and then call us if anything does not fit neatly. You might discover that your move is straightforward and book online, or that it has quirks worth discussing. You lose nothing by trying the tool first, since nothing is charged until you choose to pay.

Booking and paying after the quote

After a free quote, we confirm the booking with you and agree how you would like to pay. After an instant quote, you pay online. Our guide on how to pay for your removal explains the three online options, and they all share the same cancellation terms: free, with everything paid refunded to the original payment method.

A quick checklist before you decide

  1. Is my property a standard size with ordinary furniture? If yes, try the instant quote.
  2. Do I have anything heavy, valuable or awkward? If yes, mention it, or call.
  3. Is my date firm? If yes, you can book now. If not, a free quote, or a refundable deposit, is safer.
  4. Do I need packing? If yes, ask about a survey.
  5. Is my journey long? If five hours or more, speak to us for a confirmed price.

Questions to have ready for either route

You will get a quicker and more accurate price if you have a few facts to hand before you start. They are the same whichever way you ask.

  • The full postcodes at both ends of the move.
  • The number of bedrooms, or whether it is a studio or a flat.
  • Roughly how full the property is, including the loft, garage and garden.
  • Any large, heavy or fragile items, such as a piano, a safe or a glass table.
  • Whether there are stairs, a lift, or a long walk from where the van can park.
  • Your preferred date, and whether you could be flexible by a day or two.

Having these ready means the tool can price your move properly, and it means that if you do call, the conversation is short and useful. It is also a good habit when comparing companies, because you can give each one the same details and compare like with like.

A note on comparing prices

When you compare quotes from different companies, check that each one covers the same things. Is insurance included? Is VAT added on top? Are dismantling and reassembly of beds included? Is the price fixed, or an estimate that can change on the day? A lower figure that leaves things out is not really lower.
