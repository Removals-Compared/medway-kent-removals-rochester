What Removal Companies Wish You Knew Before Moving Day

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | November 2026

The short answer is that moving day rarely runs late because of the lifting. It runs late because of access, parking, packing that is not finished and waiting for keys. All four can be sorted before the van arrives, and this article covers what to do about each one, why it matters to the crew's timing, and what a fixed quote does and does not cover.

Why moving day runs late (and it is rarely the lifting)

A trained crew can carry a wardrobe down a staircase without much fuss. What they cannot do is carry it while the van is parked three streets away, while a drawer is still full of paperwork, or while the buyer's solicitor has not yet released the keys. Those are the moments that add hours.

The usual causes of delay are:

  • Access that was not described, such as a long carry from the van, tight stairs or restricted parking.
  • Rooms that are only half packed when the crew arrives.
  • Boxes with no labels, so nobody knows where they go at the other end.
  • Large items nobody mentioned when the quote was prepared.
  • Keys or completion arriving later than planned.

A quote is based on the contents and access you described. If the reality on the day is different, that matters, because the job was planned around the description. A prepared home means a shorter move, not a rushed one. The crew can work steadily and handle your furniture properly instead of improvising.

Tell us about access before we arrive

Access affects how a job is planned more than the number of bedrooms does. A three bedroom house with a driveway can be quicker than a two bedroom flat on the third floor with no lift and nowhere to park. When you ask for a quote, tell us about:

  • Stairs and landings: how many flights, and whether any turn sharply.
  • Narrow hallways and doorways: especially if you own a large sofa, wardrobe or American style fridge freezer.
  • Parking distance: how far a Luton van will have to stop from your door, and whether the street is usually full.
  • Permits and bays: if the road has resident bays or restrictions, these normally need arranging by you or your landlord or managing agent in advance.
  • Lifts and time restrictions: some blocks only allow lift booking for certain hours, and some have rules about moving times.

These details decide how the van is placed and how the crew is sized. A long carry from the van is slower than a short one, and a booked lift slot is a hard deadline. If we know in advance, we can plan around it. If we find out on the day, everyone loses time.

Photos help. A quick picture of the front of the property, the parking situation and any awkward staircase, sent with your quote request, tells us more than a paragraph of description.

Finish packing before the crew arrives

This is the single biggest thing within your control. When a room is packed and closed up, loading is smooth. When drawers are open, cupboards are half emptied and boxes are still being taped, the crew either waits or works around you, and things get missed or damaged.

Label by destination room

Write the room the box is going to, not what is in it. "Kitchen" or "Bedroom 2" is more useful on the day than "miscellaneous". Every price includes placing items in the correct rooms, but that only works if the boxes say where they belong. Put the label on the side as well as the top, because boxes get stacked.

Keep a bag that travels with you

Pack one bag or box that stays with you rather than going on the van. It should hold:

  • Passports, tenancy or purchase paperwork and other important documents.
  • Medicines and anything you need daily.
  • Phone chargers and a power bank.
  • Keys, spare keys and any door or gate codes.
  • A kettle, mugs, tea bags and a few basics for the first evening.

If you cannot finish everything

Some people simply run out of time, especially with work and children. If that is you, our packing services cover full or partial packing with materials, priced per job. Handing over the kitchen or the loft, the two rooms that take longest, is often more sensible than a rushed all-night session before the move.

The things that surprise people on the day

Most surprises are items that nobody thought to mention. Before you request a quote, walk the property and list anything out of the ordinary.

  • Outdoor items: sheds, heavy planters, garden furniture, barbecues and anything stored in a garage.
  • Large mirrors and glass tops: awkward to carry and worth flagging so they are protected properly.
  • Very heavy or unusual pieces: pianos, safes, gym equipment, stone statues. Tell us before the quote is given, not on the morning.
  • Loft and garage contents: these are easy to forget and can add a surprising amount of volume.

Appliances

Washing machines, dishwashers and gas cookers need to be disconnected by a qualified person before the move. Please arrange this in advance rather than assuming the crew will do it. Empty and defrost your fridge freezer at least a day beforehand, so it is dry and ready to move.

Beds and flat pack

Dismantling and reassembly of standard beds and flat pack furniture is included in every price. If something is unusual, such as a bed with a built-in mechanism, a fitted wardrobe or a custom piece, say so in advance so we can plan for it.

Completion day delays and keys

If you are buying or selling, your move date depends on completion, and completion does not always happen when you expect. Funds can arrive late and chains can slip. Build some flexibility into your day if you can, and do not make the removals crew the first to find out.

  • Tell us straight away if you hear of a delay. A phone call at 10am is far more useful than the crew standing at the door at noon.
  • Know who to call: have your solicitor's or agent's direct number in your phone, not just in an email.
  • Keep your phone charged and reachable all day. A dead phone is a surprisingly common reason for a stalled move.

A night-before and morning-of checklist

The night before

  1. Confirm parking for the van at both addresses, including any permits or bay suspensions.
  2. Clear a route from every room to the front door, including hallways and stairs.
  3. Empty and defrost the fridge freezer, and make sure appliances are disconnected.
  4. Check every box is closed and labelled with its destination room.
  5. Pack your essentials bag and put documents, medicines and chargers in it.
  6. Arrange care for pets and young children, so they are not in the way of heavy items.

The morning of the move

  1. Take final meter readings and photograph them.
  2. Keep valuables, jewellery and important papers with you.
  3. Have keys, fobs and access codes ready for both properties.
  4. Keep the van's parking space clear. Park your own car elsewhere.
  5. Have tea ready if you like. It is not essential, but it is appreciated.
  6. Before the crew leaves, walk through every room, plus the loft, shed, garage and cupboards, to make sure nothing is left behind.

Frequently asked questions

What should I have ready before the removal crew arrives?

Have everything packed, boxes closed and labelled by destination room, appliances disconnected, the fridge freezer emptied and defrosted, and a clear route to the door. Keep documents, medicines, chargers and keys in a bag that stays with you.

Do I need to be at the property while the removal team loads?

We recommend that you or someone you trust is available, so the crew can ask questions and you can do the final walk-through. If that is not possible, speak to us before the day so we can agree an arrangement.

What happens if my completion is delayed on moving day?

Tell us as soon as you hear, rather than waiting at the door. We can then talk through the options with you. Our guide to handling a delayed completion explains what to do and who to contact.

Do removal companies move items that are not packed?

Loose items, half-filled drawers and unpacked boxes slow loading and increase the risk of damage. Tell us in advance what will not be packed so we can advise. Our packing service is available for rooms you cannot finish.

Is a deposit required and what happens if I cancel?

Yes. The deposit is 10% of the quote value, paid on booking to secure your date. The remaining 90% is due on the move date. Cancellation is free and the deposit is refunded in full.
