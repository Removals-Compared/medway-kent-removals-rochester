Moving to Herne Bay: Is It Worth It in 2026?

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | November 2026

Moving to Herne Bay is worth it if you want a seaside town with a slower everyday pace and you are happy to trade some convenience for it. It is less likely to suit you if your life depends on short, easy journeys to a particular employer or a big city centre. This guide is not a promise either way. The only reliable test is to spend time there, at different times of day and in different seasons, before you commit.

The short answer: who Herne Bay suits and who it might not

The honest verdict is that it depends on what you want from where you live. A seaside town offers something different from a city or a commuter suburb, and the people who are happiest in one are not always the people who would be happiest in another.

Herne Bay may suit you if:

  • You want to live close to the sea and are happy to build your routine around it.
  • You work from home, work flexibly, or have a commute you have already tested and are comfortable with.
  • You are downsizing or relocating and want a different pace of life from the one you have now.
  • You are leaving London or another part of Kent and want to see what a coastal town offers for your budget, which you should check against current listings rather than assume.

You may want to look elsewhere if:

  • You need to be in the office five days a week and the journey is already a stretch.
  • You want a busy city centre on your doorstep, with the range of shops, venues and workplaces that comes with it.
  • You dislike quieter off-season periods in coastal towns.
  • You rely on a very specific school, hospital or employer and have not yet checked how far it is from the streets you are considering.

Visit first. Walk the seafront and the town centre, look at the streets you could actually afford to live on, and ask yourself whether you could imagine an ordinary Tuesday there, not just a sunny weekend.

What living in Herne Bay is actually like

Herne Bay is a seaside town, so the seafront shapes a lot of daily life, from how people spend weekends to how busy the town feels at different times of year. Alongside that, you will want the ordinary things to work: a food shop you can reach easily, a GP surgery you can register with, a school or nursery place if you need one, and somewhere to go on a quiet evening.

We have not listed specific shops, schools or attractions here because we would rather you checked them yourself than took our word for it. Use the council, the NHS find-a-GP tool and school inspection reports for the things that matter most to your household.

Different parts of town

Any town of this kind has areas with a different feel: streets near the sea, streets nearer the centre, and quieter residential roads further back. Walk each one rather than relying on a description. Look at noise, parking pressure, how well kept the roads are, and how far you would be from the things you use every day.

Summer and winter

Coastal towns often feel different between summer and winter. Visit once in warm weather and once on a grey weekday in the colder months if you can. If you can only visit once, pick a weekday, because that is closer to how most of your life there would be. Check how things such as seafront parking and local businesses change across the year.

Housing and what your money buys

Herne Bay has a mix of homes, as most established towns do. You are likely to see older terraced and semi-detached houses, flats, and some newer developments. Property types vary street by street, so look at what is actually for sale or to rent in the specific roads you like.

We are not quoting house prices here. They change quickly and any figure in an article would go out of date. Check current asking prices and sold prices on the main property websites, and compare like with like: the same number of bedrooms, a similar type of home and a similar distance from the sea or the centre.

How the age and type of home affect moving day

This is the part most local guides skip. The home you choose changes how your move runs:

  • Older houses can have steep or narrow staircases, tight turns and small doorways, which slow down large furniture.
  • Flats raise questions about floors, lifts, shared entrances and how far the van can park from the door.
  • Streets close to the seafront or town centre may have limited parking, so it is worth finding out in advance where a large van can stop. Check any local parking rules on the day you are moving.
  • Newer homes often have easier access, but check for tight cul-de-sacs or shared drives.

None of this is a reason to avoid a home you love. It is a reason to tell your removal company about it when you ask for a quote.

Getting around: commuting, transport and daily travel

Commuting is where many moves to a coastal town are won or lost. Herne Bay has rail and road routes towards Canterbury, the wider Kent area and London, but you should check train times, fares and road conditions yourself with current timetables and journey planners rather than relying on a general claim.

Before you commit, do this:

  • Make the journey at the real times you would travel, including the busy peak and the return trip.
  • Check what happens when there is a delay. Is there a sensible alternative route?
  • Add up the true weekly cost of travel, not just the time.
  • If you work hybrid hours, think about how many days a week you will really travel.

If you are moving from a city, think about cars too. Some households manage without one in a city but find they need one in a smaller town, or the other way round. Check where you would park, whether the home has off-street parking, and whether a permit is needed on the street.

The honest downsides to weigh up

Every place has trade-offs. Here are the common ones people weigh up with a seaside town, each framed as a question to check rather than a verdict.

  • Seasonality: does the town feel the way you want it to in the quieter months? Visit then, if you can.
  • Distance from work: is your employer, or the type of employer you may want in future, within a journey you can live with?
  • Distance from family and friends: how long does it take to see the people you see most, and will that still work in a year?
  • Choice and range: are the services, shops and activities you use now available nearby, or would you travel for them?
  • Weather and exposure: do you enjoy a coastal setting in all weathers? Think about wind and damp when you view a property, and look carefully for signs of damp indoors.
  • Access to your home: will the street, stairs and parking suit your lifestyle, and a moving van?

If most of these questions come back with comfortable answers, that is a good sign. If several worry you, that is useful to know before you have paid a deposit on a house, not after.
