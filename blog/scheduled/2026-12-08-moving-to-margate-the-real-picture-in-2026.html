Is Margate right for you? An honest overview

Margate is a coastal town in Thanet, East Kent. People who tend to enjoy living in a town like this usually share a few traits:

  • They like being near the sea and are happy with a town that changes noticeably between the busy season and the quiet months.
  • They are comfortable with period buildings and the quirks that come with them, such as narrow staircases, sloping floors and limited storage.
  • They work from home, work locally, or are prepared to plan around a longer commute rather than expecting an easy one.
  • They want a town with its own identity rather than a commuter estate.

It may be a poorer fit if you need lots of off-street parking, want a large modern home with generous storage, or rely on frequent, fast connections to London or Medway every day. A seaside town also means seasonal visitors, so streets near the front can be busier in summer than you would guess from a winter viewing.

Our advice is simple. Visit more than once, on a weekday and a weekend, in different weather. Walk the streets around any property you are considering at the time of day you would be coming home. It will tell you more than a listing will, and it will also show you the parking and access issues we discuss below.

What the housing looks like and why it matters on moving day

Seaside towns in Kent typically offer a mix of Victorian and Edwardian terraces, larger period houses that have been converted into flats, and a smaller share of newer houses and apartment blocks. If you are looking at Margate, expect to see several of these. Each one affects how a removal day runs.

Period terraces

Terraced houses often have narrow hallways, steep or turning staircases and rooms that are smaller than modern equivalents. A large sofa or wardrobe that sat happily in a newer home may need to be turned, tilted or taken apart to get upstairs. Many terraces also front directly onto the street, which means the van parks on the road rather than on a drive.

Converted flats

Flats in converted period buildings can mean several flights of stairs, shared entrances and communal landings. Upper floors with no lift add real time to a move because every item is carried by hand. Tell your removal company which floor you are on at both ends, and whether there is a lift, so the crew is sized sensibly for the job.

Newer homes and blocks

Newer properties usually have wider doors and straighter stairs, but they can come with their own restrictions, such as lift booking slots or rules about where vans can stop. Ask your landlord, managing agent or building manager before moving day.

Parking and loading

Narrow streets, resident parking and one-way roads can all make it harder to get a Luton van close to the door. Some practical tips:

  • Look at the street at the time of day you plan to move and note where a large van could realistically stop.
  • Check with the council about whether a parking suspension is needed and how much notice is required. Do this early, because notice periods vary.
  • Ask neighbours not to block the space you have reserved, and consider leaving a note on cars nearby.
  • Tell us about any long carry from the van to the front door, because that changes how the job is planned.

Measure before you commit to keeping it

Before you decide which furniture is coming with you, measure it against the property it is going into. Do this for each large item:

  1. Measure the height, width and depth of the item, including legs, arms and any overhang.
  2. Measure the front door, internal doors, hallway width and any tight turns at the bottom or top of the stairs.
  3. Check the headroom on the stairs and landing, not just the width.
  4. For sofas, check whether the arms or legs come off, and whether the item can be tilted on end.
  5. Take photos of the stairwell and awkward corners to share with your removal company.

Standard beds and flat pack furniture are dismantled and reassembled as part of every price we quote. Solid, pre-assembled pieces are another matter. If something will not fit, you are better off knowing before moving day than discovering it on the doorstep.

Moving from London or Medway to Margate: planning the logistics

A longer move changes the planning in a few ways, even when it stays within Kent or crosses into it from London.

  • Earlier start. Moves with more travel between the two addresses need a morning start so that unloading is not pushed late into the evening. Be ready to begin first thing.
  • Booking lead time. Popular dates, such as Fridays and month ends, fill up first. Book as soon as your completion or tenancy date is confirmed.
  • Loading order. The van is loaded so that what you need first comes off last. Boxes for the kitchen and bedrooms, and essentials like kettles and bedding, are best labelled and packed where the crew can see them.
  • Pricing. The ranges above apply to moves within Thanet or to neighbouring East Kent towns. A move that starts in Medway or London is quoted individually, so give us both addresses and we will provide a fixed price.

A practical timeline: from viewing to first week in Margate

Several weeks before

  • Declutter room by room. Anything you do not need is something you do not pay to move, and period properties rarely have spare storage.
  • Request quotes. Remember that each quote is valid for 14 days, so time your requests to match your decision point.
  • Once your date is confirmed, book it and pay the 10% deposit to secure the slot. For more on timing, read how far in advance to book.

The final week

  • Pack non-essentials first and keep an essentials box for the first night.
  • Sort parking at both ends, and arrange any suspension or permission needed.
  • Check access at both addresses: lifts, keys, entry codes, steps, narrow turns, and any times you are not allowed to move in or out.
  • Confirm the plan with your removal company, including anything that has changed since the quote.

Moving day

  • Have keys, entry codes and parking sorted before the crew arrives.
  • Keep valuables, documents and medication with you rather than in the van.
  • The crew will protect furniture with blankets, load the van, transport everything, unload and place items in the correct rooms. Walk through each room with them at the start so they know where everything goes.
  • Have the 90% balance ready for the move date. We accept all major credit and debit cards, bank transfer, cash and cryptocurrency.

The first week

  • Update your address with your bank, employer, insurers, the DVLA and any subscriptions.
  • Set up or transfer utilities, broadband and council tax. Take meter readings on arrival.
  • Register with a local GP and dentist if you are moving from outside the area.
  • Redirect your post and tell friends and family where you now live.

Mistakes people make when moving to a coastal town

  • Underestimating parking and access. The most common problem is not telling the removal company about steps, upper floors or a long carry from the van. A quote is only as accurate as the information behind it.
  • Keeping too much furniture. Pieces that suited a larger or newer home can overwhelm a smaller or older one. Measure first, then decide.
  • Leaving the booking late. Good dates go quickly. Booking early gives you choice and less stress.
  • Not checking the insurance. Ask every company what cover is included. Ours includes goods in transit insurance at full replacement value and public liability insurance in every price.
  • Ignoring the off-season. Visit in winter as well as summer, so you know what the town is like all year.
