Is Your Removals Deposit Refundable? What to Check

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | October 2026

Almost every removal company asks for something up front to hold your date. Whether you call it a deposit, a booking fee or a retainer, the question people really want answered is the same: if my plans change, do I get that money back? The honest answer is that it depends entirely on the company you book with, which is why it is worth asking before you pay anything.

This guide explains what a removals deposit is actually for, the different ways companies treat it, the questions to ask, and how refunds work with Medway and Kent Removals.

What a deposit is for

A removal is booked against a particular day. On that day a crew, a vehicle and a place in the diary are set aside for you, and that is a day the company cannot sell to anyone else. A deposit is a small payment that shows both sides are serious. It protects the company from empty days, and it protects you by turning a vague promise into a booking.

A deposit is not meant to be a profit centre, and it is not meant to be a penalty. It is a part payment of the price you have already agreed. If you move as planned, it is simply the first slice of the bill, and it comes off what you owe on the day.

How deposits are usually treated

There is no single industry rule, so you will meet several different approaches. It helps to know them so that you can spot which one you are being offered.

  • Fully refundable: you can cancel and get everything back. This is the simplest and the fairest from the customer's side.
  • Refundable up to a deadline: you get the money back if you cancel by a certain date, and lose it after that. This is common, but the deadline needs to be written down and easy to find.
  • Non-refundable: the deposit is kept whatever happens. Some companies do this, and it is legal if it is clearly explained, but it leaves you carrying the risk if a house sale falls through.
  • Not described at all: the most worrying category. If you cannot find a cancellation policy, assume you will have a dispute later.

The point is not that one of these is always wrong. The point is that you should know which one applies before you part with money, not after.

Why it matters more for house moves

Moving dates are less certain than most things we book. A house move depends on a chain: your buyer, your seller, two sets of solicitors, mortgage offers and surveys. Any link can slip, and a date that looked fixed on Monday can move by weeks by Friday.

That is why a refundable deposit has real value. If your completion date is not yet exchanged, the ability to cancel without losing money is worth more than a small saving on the price. If you have already exchanged contracts, the date is far more certain and the question matters less, but it is still worth asking.

The questions to ask any removal company

Whoever you book with, ask these before you pay and keep the answers in writing. An email is enough.

  1. How much is the deposit, and is it a share of the price or a flat amount?
  2. Is it refundable if I cancel, and by what date?
  3. If I move my date, what happens to the deposit?
  4. Does the deposit come off my final bill?
  5. Will I get a written confirmation showing the price, the date and what I have paid?
  6. What happens if you cancel or cannot make the date?

The last question is the one people forget. A good company will tell you plainly what it does when the problem is on its side, and it will not make that sound like an awkward thing to ask.

Warning signs

Most removal companies are honest. A few habits are worth treating with caution, though, because they appear again and again in complaints.

  • A large deposit: asking for a big share of the price long before the move is a risk to you. A modest percentage is normal.
  • Pressure to pay now: a real company will hold a quote for a reasonable time. Our own quotes stay valid for 14 days, subject to availability.
  • Cash only, no receipt: you should always get a receipt or a written confirmation for money you hand over.
  • A price that changes after payment: your quote should be fixed, and any extras should be agreed with you first.
  • No company details: check that a company number, an address area and a real phone line are published.

Paying by card gives you extra protection

How you pay matters as well as who you pay. Paying a deposit by credit or debit card can give you more routes to get your money back if something goes wrong than a bank transfer to a stranger would. If the company vanishes or fails to deliver, a card payment is easier to raise with your card provider. Bank transfers and cash leave you with fewer options, so use them only with a company you have checked.

What to keep

After you pay, save three things in one place: the confirmation email, the quote showing the fixed price, and the receipt. If anything is disputed later, those three documents settle most arguments quickly. When you book through our tool you receive a confirmation by email straight away, with the move details, the addresses, what you have paid and what is left to pay on the day.

What if you are not sure of your date yet?

If you do not know your date, you have two sensible options. You can ask for a free quote first and book only when your date is firm. Or you can book online with the refundable deposit, knowing you can cancel for free if the date falls through. What you should avoid is paying a non-refundable deposit for a date that depends on a chain you do not control.

It is also worth telling the company about any uncertainty at the start. We would rather know that your completion might slip, because we can often suggest a flexible arrangement or keep a nearby date in mind. It costs nothing to ask, and it can save a great deal of stress later.
