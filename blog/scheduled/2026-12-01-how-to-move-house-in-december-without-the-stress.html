How to Move House in December Without the Stress

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | December 2026

The calmest way to move house in December is to agree your date early, work backwards from it with a simple timeline, pack your Christmas things separately, and plan moving day around short daylight and wet weather. Do those four things and the move stops competing with the festive period. This guide is built around the December calendar, with a plan for before the move, on the day and in the first days in your new home.

What is different about moving in December

A December move is the same job as any other, but a few things change the way you plan it.

  • Daylight is short. In the middle of winter you have roughly eight hours of usable light. Loading, driving and unloading all need to fit inside that, so start early and keep the order of the day tight. Nobody wants the last sofa carried up an unlit path.
  • Weather affects access. Wet pavements, icy driveways and leaves on the path make carrying slower and riskier. Think about where the van can stand, how far it is to the front door and what the ground will be like at both ends.
  • Dates close to Christmas can be limited. Plenty of people want to be settled before the 25th, so the sooner you agree a date, the more choice you have. We cannot promise any particular date in December, which is why asking early matters.
  • There can be upsides. Some people find the weeks before Christmas feel less crowded than a summer weekend. That depends on the year and the date, so treat it as a possibility rather than something to rely on.

A December timeline: what to do and when

Count back from your move date, not from the start of the month. If you move in the week before Christmas, "four to six weeks before" means early to mid November, so a December move is often decided by what you do in November. Copy this list and tick things off.

Four to six weeks before

  • Confirm your completion or tenancy date with your solicitor, agent or landlord, and ask how firm it is.
  • Get a fixed quote and book the move to secure the date.
  • Start decluttering. Fewer boxes means a shorter day and a lighter load.
  • Book time off work if you need it, and check whether your employer has a shutdown over the festive period.

Three weeks before

  • Order or collect packing materials: boxes, paper, tape, marker pens.
  • Pack rarely used rooms first: spare bedroom, loft, garage, books, off-season clothes.
  • Sort Christmas decorations, wrapping paper, cards and gifts already bought into their own clearly labelled boxes.
  • Decide what you will carry yourself, such as children's presents and valuable gifts.

Two weeks before

  • Update your address with your bank, employer, doctor, insurers and subscriptions.
  • Arrange post redirection.
  • Tell schools, nurseries and your employer about the move date.
  • Check parking and access at both ends. Ask neighbours if you need space for the van, and consider whether a bay or a narrow road could cause delays.

The week before

  • Run down and defrost the freezer, and plan meals around what is left.
  • Pack an essentials bag for each person: medication, chargers, toiletries, a change of clothes.
  • Confirm arrival times with your removal company and with whoever holds the keys.
  • Decide where Christmas deliveries should go. Online orders and parcels from relatives are easy to lose track of, so choose a delivery address, or a neighbour or collection point, and tell senders.
  • Check the forecast and think about how you will protect floors and doorways.

Packing in December without losing Christmas

The trick is to pack in the order you will need things, with Christmas items last in and first out.

  • Label Christmas boxes clearly. Use a bold marker and write "CHRISTMAS" on at least two sides, with the room you want them in. Decorations, wrapping paper, cards and spare lights belong here.
  • Keep presents out of the van if you can. Children's gifts are best hidden and moved by you, in the car or at a relative's house. The same goes for anything fragile or valuable, such as jewellery, electronics and sentimental items.
  • Protect fragile festive items. Glass baubles, ceramic ornaments and light-up decorations need wrapping individually, with plenty of paper and no heavy items on top. Put the lightest boxes on top of the stack.
  • Pack a first night box. Fill it with a kettle, mugs, tea and coffee, milk or a long-life alternative, bedding, towels, chargers, medication, toiletries, a torch and children's comfort items such as a favourite toy or book. Mark it clearly and make sure it is unloaded first or travels with you.

Moving day in cold, wet and dark conditions

A good plan for the day matters more in December than at any other time of year.

  • Start early and set the order. Agree an arrival time, have the essentials already packed and clear a route through the house. Decide which rooms are loaded first, so the last items out are small and easy to carry, not a wardrobe in fading light.
  • Protect floors and doorways. Wet shoes and boxes make hallways slippery. Old towels, door mats or spare rugs near the entrance help, and so do dry cloths to hand for wiping down. Keep walkways and steps clear, and sweep or salt paths if there is ice. Talk to your crew about what they need at each address.
  • Dress for the day. Wear layers, sturdy footwear and gloves. Keep a flask of hot drinks and some snacks for everyone helping.
  • Keep children and pets safe. Open doors, heavy furniture and a busy driveway are not the place for either. A relative, friend or neighbour can look after them for the day.
  • Check the new home before you arrive. Make sure the heating, lights and water are on, and take meter readings on arrival. Keep a torch and phone chargers in your essentials bag.

Furniture blankets and protection are included in every quote, so your furniture is wrapped and padded while it travels. That helps against damp and knocks, but no one can control the weather, so do not expect a heavy downpour to be harmless. Allow a little extra time and keep boxes off wet ground where you can.

If completion is delayed close to Christmas

Chains do slip in December, and it is worth thinking about it before it happens.

  • Stay in touch. Ask your solicitor and agent for regular updates, and tell your removal company as soon as you know the date is at risk.
  • Know what the cost is. Cancellation is free and your deposit is refunded in full, so a failed date does not mean losing money. Be aware that a replacement date depends on availability, and the days near Christmas can be limited.
  • Consider storage. If you must leave your old home before you can enter the new one, short term storage may help. It is priced per week by volume, and you would need to ask about timing and arrangements.
  • Have a fallback. Keep a small bag packed with what you need for a few nights, and decide in advance whether you could stay with family if the date moves.

Settling in before Christmas Day

You do not need to unpack everything by the 25th. Aim for a home that feels comfortable.

  1. Set up beds first, so everyone sleeps properly, starting with the children's rooms.
  2. Get the kitchen working: kettle, basic cooking kit, food for the first few days.
  3. Unpack the Christmas box. Putting the tree and lights up early is good for morale, even if the room around it is still full of boxes.
  4. Leave low-priority rooms such as the loft, garage and spare room until January.
  5. Update your address where you have not already, and check that your post redirection is working and parcels are going to the right place.
  6. Find your new bin and recycling collection days. These can change over the festive period, and they are different in every area, so check with the local council.

Plan a simple Christmas meal, and give yourself permission to leave some boxes sealed until the new year.
