New Year, New Home: Is January a Good Time to Move in Kent?

By Amos Osho, Founder of Medway and Kent Removals | December 2026

Is January a good month to move in Kent?

It can be, as long as you plan for short days and unpredictable weather. January is not the easiest month to move, but it is often a practical one. Many tenancies end, jobs change and sales complete at the start of the year, and a move after Christmas gives you a clean break before spring.

It suits people whose dates are already fixed, such as a tenancy ending or a completion booked in, and people who want to be settled before the spring rush of listings and moves. It suits you less well if you have a tight budget with no room for a delay, if your move depends on a long chain that may slip, or if you cannot be flexible about the day.

This article is deliberately balanced. It covers what really changes in January, how to deal with each problem, a week by week plan, moving day tips, what a move costs in Medway and how to book. If you want general cold weather advice, we also have a separate post with tips for cold weather removals in Kent. This one is narrower and focuses on the decision to move in January and on timing.

The real advantages of a January move

A natural point for a fresh start

After Christmas, many people are already clearing cupboards, sorting through gifts and rethinking how they live. That makes it a good time to declutter before a move. Less to pack means less to load, and fewer boxes to unpack at the other end.

Dates that already fall in January

A lot of moves are not a free choice. Tenancy agreements often run to the end of December or the start of January. Job changes frequently begin in the new year. Families may want to change address at the start of a school term rather than part way through one. If your date falls in January, the question is not whether to move but how to do it well.

Possible flexibility on dates

Some people find they have more choice over the exact day at this time of year. We cannot promise that, and we cannot guarantee availability for any particular date, so the sensible approach is to ask early and see what is open. There is no January offer or discount, so please do not plan around one.

Settling in before spring

Many households list and move in spring. If you move in January, you can be unpacked, registered with local services and familiar with the area before things get busy. You also have time to sort out small jobs in the new home, such as shelves, curtains and broadband, without feeling rushed.

The downsides, and how to deal with them

Short daylight

The biggest practical change in January is how little daylight you have. Loading, driving and unloading all take time, and working in the dark is harder and slower, especially on unfamiliar steps and paths.

  • Plan for an early start so that the main work is done while it is light.
  • Keep the loading order simple. Get the bedroom and kitchen essentials, and anything you will need first, in a sensible place so they come off the van when you need them.
  • Do your own packing well ahead, so that nothing is still being taped up when the crew arrives.
  • Check that outside lights work at both addresses, and keep a couple of torches to hand.

Rain, frost and ice

Wet and icy surfaces are a safety issue for the crew and for you. Before moving day, look at the paths, drives, steps and any shared entrances at both ends. Clear leaves and standing water, and put down grit or salt where frost is likely. If you have a steep drive or a tight access road, tell us when you ask for a quote, because access affects how a move is planned.

If the forecast worries you, contact us as early as you can rather than on the morning itself, so that the options can be talked through.

Slow chains and winter illness

Sales can slip at any time of year, but holiday closures and winter illness can slow solicitors, lenders and estate agents in the first weeks of the year. Nobody can remove that uncertainty. What you can do is choose a booking that does not punish you for a delay. How to plan a January move week by week

Four to six weeks before

  • Declutter after Christmas. Take unwanted gifts, outgrown clothes and unused kitchenware to charity shops or the tip before you start packing.
  • Get quotes. Ask for a fixed quote online or by phone, and describe the property honestly, including stairs, parking and any large or awkward items.
  • Check our guide on how far in advance to book a removal company if you are unsure about timing.

Quotes are valid for 14 days from the date issued and are subject to availability. That matters in January because exchange dates and tenancy end dates can move. Try to time your request so that the quote is still valid when your dates are confirmed, and do not leave it so late that the date you want has gone. If your quote expires, ask for it to be refreshed.

Two weeks before

  • Confirm access and parking at both addresses. If a space needs reserving or a neighbour needs warning, do it now.
  • Arrange meter readings and utility handovers, and take dated photos of the meters at the old and new homes.
  • Book any cleaning, and check keys and collection times with your agent or landlord.
  • Start packing rooms you use least. A moving house checklist will help you keep track.

Moving week

Pack a bag or box of essentials for the first night, and keep it with you rather than on the van. In January it is worth including:

  • Bedding and towels for everyone
  • A kettle, mugs, tea, coffee and some simple food
  • Chargers, medication and washing things
  • Torches and spare batteries
  • Warm clothes, and anything you need to heat the new home, such as a portable heater if the boiler is not yet checked

Check that the heating and hot water work at the new address before moving day if you can, and find the stopcock and fuse box.

Moving day in winter: practical tips

  • Protect floors and doorways. Wet shoes and muddy boots are unavoidable. Lay old mats, towels or cardboard in the hall and at the door, and keep a mop and towel handy. Furniture is protected with blankets as part of the price, but floor protection is something to arrange yourself.
  • Clear and salt paths and steps. Do this before the crew arrives and again if it freezes later in the day. Check the lighting at both addresses.
  • Keep children, pets and valuables in a warm, separate room. It keeps them safe, out of the way of heavy items and open doors, and it keeps the house warmer for the people who are not carrying.
  • Keep important items with you. Documents, jewellery, keys and medication should travel in your own car.
