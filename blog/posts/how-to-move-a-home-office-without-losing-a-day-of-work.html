How to Move a Home Office Without Losing a Day of Work

Published 6 Oct 2026  |  9 min read  |  Medway & Kent Removals

You can protect the work that matters, but a house move rarely leaves zero disruption. The realistic aim is to keep your important calls, deadlines and client access intact, pack one small separate kit that gets you working within an hour of arriving, and treat moving day as a light day with the day after as your proper return. The plan below runs from two weeks before to your first working day in the new place.

Decide what "not losing a day" means for you

Before you pack anything, work out which days and tasks cannot slip. For most home workers that is a short list: a client call, a deadline, a shift that needs a live connection, or access to a system that only works from one place.

  • Write down every call, deadline and commitment in the week of the move and the week after.
  • Mark which ones need a proper connection and a quiet space, and which could be done from a phone.
  • Consider booking leave or a lighter schedule for moving day itself. Treat the day after as the realistic return to full work.
  • Tell clients and colleagues your dates, and give them a fallback contact in case you are hard to reach.

Doing this early makes every later decision easier, because you know what you are protecting.

Two weeks before: broadband, backups and a shortlist of kit

These three jobs have the longest lead times, so start with them.

Broadband

Confirm the start date at the new address and the end date at the old one. Then ask your provider directly what happens if there is a gap between the two. Switch-over dates depend on the provider and the line, so treat any date as a target rather than a promise, and keep the confirmation email somewhere you can find it on the day.

A fallback connection

Set up a mobile data or hotspot fallback now, not on the day you need it. Check your phone plan allows tethering, then test it with a proper video call, because a connection that loads a web page can still struggle with a meeting. If your work uses a lot of data, check your allowance too.

Backups and a kit list

Check your cloud backups are actually running and that recent files appear online. Then list the devices, chargers and cables you cannot work without. This list becomes the contents of your minimum viable desk, covered next.

Build a "minimum viable desk" kit

The idea is simple. Instead of hoping your office is unpacked quickly, you pack a small, separate kit that lets you do the essential part of your job within an hour of arriving. Everything else can wait.

Pack one clearly labelled box or bag containing:

  • Your laptop and its charger
  • A headset or earbuds
  • The broadband router and its power lead
  • A power strip or extension lead
  • Your phone charger and any adaptor you rely on
  • A notebook, pens and any security token or key you need to log in

Label it clearly as the office kit and keep it apart from the rest of the boxes. The safest option is to keep it with you in your own car. If you would rather it travels on the van, ask us beforehand whether it can be loaded last and unloaded first, so we can agree how to handle it.

Before you unplug anything, take photos of the cable layouts behind your desk and label each cable with tape. It takes ten minutes and saves a lot of guesswork when you are tired and surrounded by boxes.

The week before: tell people and deal with awkward furniture

A week out, send a short note to clients and colleagues confirming your moving day, your lighter availability and a fallback contact. People are usually understanding when they have notice and a clear return date.

This is also the time to look at your desk and any office furniture honestly. Measure the desk, shelving or filing cabinets against the doorways and stairs at both addresses. Large or solid pieces sometimes go in through a door but will not come out again, or the other way round. Standard flat pack desks and shelving are dismantled and reassembled as part of the removal price. For anything larger or non-standard, such as a heavy executive desk or a sectional bookcase, tell us before the move so it can be discussed and noted in your quote. If a piece will not fit at the new house, it is better to decide now whether to sell it, donate it or replace it than to find out on the day.

If your paperwork is bulky, start sorting. Archive or shred what you no longer need, and separate current client files from everything else. Packing fewer boxes means a faster move, and our packing service is available if you would rather hand the packing over so you can keep working.

The night before: backup, shutdown and packing the rest

The evening before is for locking things down.

  1. Run a full manual backup of your work files, then open a few files from the backup to check it actually worked.
  2. Pack monitors, drives and peripherals with padding. Use the original boxes if you still have them. If not, wrap screens in something soft and pack them upright rather than flat, with nothing heavy on top.
  3. Label everything by room and by priority. Office boxes should say where they go in the new house, so they can be placed in the correct room.
  4. Keep sensitive items with you. If confidentiality matters, take client papers, laptops and external drives in your own vehicle rather than on the van. If your clients have rules about how their data is handled, check them rather than assuming.
  5. Charge your phone, laptop and any power bank so your fallback connection is ready.

Moving day: protecting the office and the schedule

Brief the crew at the start. Tell them which boxes and items are the office kit, which room they go in at the new place, and which things you are carrying yourself. A two minute conversation here is worth more than any label.

Keep your phone charged and your schedule light. Even a well run move has waiting time, and your attention will be needed for decisions. If you have a call you truly cannot move, plan to take it from your fallback connection, or from a quiet spot away from the van and the boxes.

If completion is delayed

Delays happen, and they are outside your removal firm's control. Agree a fallback in advance, such as working for a few hours from a café, a library or a friend's home. Your minimum viable desk kit makes this easy, because the laptop, charger and headset are already in one bag. We have more detail in our guide on what to do if completion is delayed.

The first working day at the new place

Give yourself the first hour for setup, and follow a fixed order so you are not troubleshooting everything at once.

  1. Power: plug in the power strip and check it works.
  2. Router: connect it, or switch to your hotspot if the line is not live yet.
  3. Laptop: log in and check email and your key systems.
  4. Screens and peripherals: add monitors, keyboard, mouse and any docking station, using your cable photos.

Before your first meeting of the day, test calls, your VPN if you use one, and the printer. It is much better to find a problem at 8.30am than at the start of a client call.

Leave the unpacking of books, files and decor until after work hours. A bare desk that works beats a beautiful office that does not. Expect to do a proper tidy over the following days, and be kind to yourself if the first day is slower than normal.

Insurance, cost and booking points for a home office

Insurance. Goods in transit insurance at full replacement value is included in every removal price, along with public liability insurance. Whether that explicitly covers work equipment such as laptops and monitors, and whether there are any limits or exclusions, is worth confirming with us in writing before moving day. Booking. If a particular weekday matters to you, book early. Same-day moves are possible subject to availability, but they are not something to rely on when you have work commitments.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really move house without losing a working day?

Often you can protect the work that matters, but it is unrealistic to promise zero disruption. Plan for a light moving day and a proper return the day after, with a minimum viable desk kit and a fallback connection to cover your essential tasks.

What should I do about broadband if there is a gap between addresses?

Ask your provider two weeks ahead what happens if the old line ends before the new one starts. Switch-over dates depend on the provider, so have a mobile hotspot ready and tested, and plan a few hours at a café, library or friend's home if you need a stable connection.

Is it safer to take my computer in my own car or on the removal van?

Keeping your laptop, external drives and sensitive papers with you is the simplest option, especially if confidentiality matters. Your equipment is also immediately to hand if you need to work. Larger items such as monitors can travel on the van if they are well padded.

Is my work equipment covered by goods in transit insurance?

Goods in transit insurance at full replacement value is included in every price. Please check with us in writing whether it covers laptops, monitors and other work equipment, and ask about any limits or exclusions. It is also worth checking any business or home policy you already hold.

Will the removal crew dismantle and rebuild my desk?

Dismantling and reassembly of standard beds and flat pack furniture, including typical flat pack desks and shelving, is included in the price. For larger or non-standard pieces, tell us before the move so it can be noted in your quote.
