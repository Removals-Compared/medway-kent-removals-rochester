Moving House in Autumn: The Advantages Nobody Tells You

Published 13 Oct 2026  |  10 min read  |  Medway & Kent Removals

Yes, autumn can be a sensible time to move house in Medway and Kent, especially if you have some say over your date. It is not automatically better than summer, and for some households it is worse. This guide sets out what really changes between September and November, what to do about each change, and where summer or spring is still the better choice.

Why summer is not automatically the best time to move

Summer is the default for good reasons. The school holidays give families a clear gap, the days are long and the ground is dry. But those same reasons are why so many people choose it, and that means more competition for the dates you want, from removal firms, conveyancers and van hire alike.

Autumn suits some moves better than others. A couple renting with a flexible tenancy, a family who would rather not move just before a new school year, or a seller whose house has been on the market since the spring may all find it works well. A family with a child about to start Year 7, or a buyer who must complete before a particular deadline, may not have the choice.

Below we look at availability, comfort on the day, schools, the property market and the downsides, then finish with a timeline and checklist you can use for an October or November move.

Easier booking and more choice of dates

Outside the school holidays there is generally less pressure on removal diaries, so you may find it easier to get the date and the start time you want. That is a general pattern, not a promise for any particular week. The only way to know is to ask for your date, and we respond to every enquiry within 60 minutes, 7 days a week.

Even so, book early. Month-end dates and Fridays are popular at any time of year, because that is when many completions fall. Cooler weather makes the physical work easier

This is the advantage people underestimate. Carrying wardrobes, washing machines and boxes of books up and down stairs is hard work on a hot July afternoon. In a cool October or November it is noticeably less tiring, for you as well as for the crew. A tired household makes more mistakes, such as a forgotten box in the loft or a missed meter reading, so a more comfortable day helps the whole move.

A few practical tips:

  • Keep a kettle, mugs, tea, coffee and some snacks in a box marked "open first" that travels in your own car.
  • Keep drinks accessible for everyone on the day. Cool weather still means a lot of physical effort.
  • Wear layers. Doors will be open for hours, so the house gets cold, but you will warm up quickly when carrying.
  • Put children's and pets' comfort items, such as blankets, a favourite toy and a bed, in your car rather than the van.

The autumn downsides and how to handle them

Autumn has real drawbacks, and it is better to plan around them than to ignore them.

Shorter days

Daylight shortens noticeably through October and November, and the clocks go back at the end of October. A move that finishes comfortably in daylight in July can run into the dark in November, which makes stairs, driveways and unfamiliar rooms harder to deal with. Aim to start early, keep the move to daylight where you can, and agree the start time with us when you book. Pack a torch or two, and make sure the lights work at both addresses.

Rain and wet leaves

Some rain is likely at some point in autumn, and wet leaves on paths and driveways are a slip risk. You can reduce the problems:

  • Sweep leaves from paths, steps and the drive the day before.
  • Put doormats or old towels down inside both front doors to protect floors and stop people slipping.
  • Keep a towel and some bin bags to hand for wet boxes and coats.
  • Do not leave cardboard boxes standing outside. Load them straight from the doorway.
  • Ask the people who own the new property, or the landlord, if there is any way to protect carpets in the hall.

Heating and lighting in the new home

In summer, a cold house is an inconvenience. In November it is miserable, especially with children. Before moving day, check that the boiler fires up, the radiators warm through and the lights work in every room. If the property has been empty, ask the seller or landlord when the heating was last used. Take meter readings on both sides of the move, and make sure the energy supply is set up in your name from the day you get the keys.

A backup plan if completion is delayed

Delays happen at any time of year, but a delay in the autumn can push you toward winter weather and shorter days. Keep a small bag of essentials ready, know where you could stay for a night if you had to, and tell your removal firm as soon as you hear of a problem. Free cancellation with the deposit refunded in full means a booked date is not a trap, but a new date will depend on availability, so the sooner we know the better. Moving with children in the school year

A summer move often means a new school starting in September, with everything new at once. An autumn move can be gentler, particularly if you move after the new term has settled. Some families choose a weekend near half-term, so children have a few days to adjust. School half-term dates vary between schools in Kent, so check with your school rather than relying on a general date.

The other side of this is that a mid-term move means a new classroom partway through the year, with friendships and routines already formed. Before you commit to a date, check admissions and whether your preferred school has a place. If you are moving out of catchment, find out when the change would take effect. For practical ideas on involving children and keeping routines steady, see our guide to moving house with children.

Buying and selling in autumn

Some sellers who are still listed in autumn may be more willing to negotiate, because they have been on the market for a while and want to move before winter. That is a possibility, not a rule, and it will not apply to every property. Do not plan your budget around it.

What matters far more than the season is the chain and the completion date. A move that depends on four other households is only as reliable as the weakest link. Talk to your solicitor and estate agent about where your chain stands and how firm the dates are before you book anything you cannot move.

A simple autumn moving timeline

This is a rough plan to work back from your completion date. Adapt it to your situation, and see our moving house checklist for Medway residents for a fuller list.

6 to 8 weeks before

  • Request quotes and book your removal date. Remember that quotes are valid for 14 days.
  • Start decluttering. Sell, donate or take to the tip what you will not use in the new home.
  • Decide whether you want packing help or will pack yourself.
  • Check school admissions if you are moving with children.

2 weeks before

  • Start packing rooms you rarely use, and label every box by room.
  • Give notice to utility providers, and arrange for supply at the new address.
  • Begin changing your address with banks, employers, doctors, insurers and the DVLA.
  • Arrange broadband early, as installation can take time.
  • Confirm your arrival time and any parking arrangements with us.

The week of the move

  • Pack an essentials bag for each person, plus the "open first" box.
  • Defrost the freezer and empty the fridge, if appliances are going in the van.
  • Take final meter readings and photograph them.
  • Confirm the completion time with your solicitor.
  • Keep phones charged and the removal team's number saved.

Autumn moving-day checklist

  • Heating tested and working at the new home.
  • Lights working, with a torch handy in case they are not.
  • Leaves swept from the drive, steps and paths at both addresses.
  • Doormats, towels and bin bags ready for wet boxes and shoes.
  • Parking space for the van kept clear.
  • Kettle, mugs, warm clothes and snacks within reach.
  • Start time agreed so the move is done in daylight where possible.

Where autumn is not the better choice

Autumn is the wrong fit if you have a child starting a new secondary school next September and need a settled base, if you want the longest possible daylight for a large or awkward move, or if your property has difficult access that will be harder in the wet. In those cases summer or spring may suit you better. The right time is the one that fits your completion date, your family and your property, and sometimes that is simply the date you are given.
