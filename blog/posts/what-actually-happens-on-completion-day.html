What Actually Happens on Completion Day: A Timeline

Published 22 Sep 2026  |  9 min read  |  Medway & Kent Removals

On completion day, the buyer's money is sent to the seller's solicitor, ownership of the property passes, and only then are the keys released. For many sales this happens somewhere between late morning and mid afternoon, but there is no guaranteed time. Plan for a window, not an exact minute.

This guide walks through the day from both sides, explains why keys are sometimes late and who to call, and sets out what a removal crew can and cannot do while you wait. It also covers what to do if completion slips. Conveyancing practice varies from sale to sale, so treat this as a general picture and check the details with your solicitor.

Exchange versus completion: what each day means

Exchange is when the sale becomes legally binding. Both sides sign matching contracts, a deposit is usually paid, and the completion date is fixed. Pulling out after exchange can have serious financial consequences, which is why many people confirm their removal booking at this stage.

Completion is the later day when the rest of the money is transferred and ownership passes to the buyer. Until it has happened, you cannot normally collect keys or move in. In some sales exchange and completion fall on the same day, but often there is a gap of days or weeks. Your solicitor will confirm the arrangements for your own sale.

A realistic timeline of completion day

No two completions run identically, but most follow the same shape.

Early morning

Solicitors on both sides check that everything is ready. The buyer's solicitor confirms that the mortgage funds, if there is a mortgage, and the buyer's own money are in place or on their way. At the old property, the seller is finishing packing and the removal crew may already be loading.

Mid-morning to afternoon

The buyer's solicitor sends the money, usually by bank transfer, to the seller's solicitor. If the seller is also buying, the money they receive is passed on to the next person up the chain. In a chain of several sales, the money may move through several sets of hands, each one waiting for the one before.

Confirmation and key release

When the seller's solicitor confirms they have received the money, they authorise release of the keys. Many contracts set a cut-off time, often around 2pm, after which completion may be pushed to the next working day. The wording differs between sales, so ask your solicitor what applies to yours. The estate agent then tells the buyer that the keys are ready.

Afterwards

The buyer collects the keys and the new home is theirs. For the seller, once funds are confirmed the property is no longer theirs, so the keys should already be with the agent and the house should be empty and clean.

Late completions happen even when everyone is doing their job properly. That is why a sensible plan has some slack in it. If you want to see how the rest of the day fits around this, there is an hour-by-hour moving day guide.

Why completion runs late, and why chains matter

In a chain, your completion depends on other people's completions. One late payment anywhere in the chain holds up everyone below it, and your solicitor can only chase what they can see. A buyer at the bottom of the chain can be fully ready and still wait for money that has not yet reached them.

Common causes of delay include:

  • A bank transfer that is sent later than planned or takes time to arrive.
  • A missing signature or a document that needs correcting.
  • A mortgage lender releasing funds later than expected.
  • A problem at another sale in the chain, which you may only hear about second hand.

Why a Friday delay often means Monday

Bank transfers generally do not clear at weekends. If the money has not been sent and received in time on a Friday, completion usually cannot happen until the next working day, which is often Monday. Bank holidays work the same way. This is why a late-afternoon problem on a Friday matters much more than the same problem on a Wednesday. Ask your solicitor how this would work for your sale, and if you can choose, a mid-week completion gives you more room if something slips.

How you get the keys and who to call

Keys are normally released by the seller's estate agent once the seller's solicitor confirms the funds have arrived. The estate agent, rather than the solicitor, is usually the person holding them, although arrangements can differ, so confirm yours in advance.

  • Buyers: keep your solicitor's direct number and the agent's direct number to hand. Agree with your solicitor how they will tell you that completion has happened, for example a phone call or a text, and who you should ring if you have heard nothing by a certain time.
  • Sellers: leave the keys with the agent in advance if you can, so you are not needed at the property for the handover. Leave the home clean and empty, and take final meter readings.

Chasing too early rarely helps. A sensible approach is to ask your solicitor roughly when they expect to send or receive the money, and then to ring at that point rather than every half hour.

What to do with the removal crew while you wait

Tell your removal company your expected key time as soon as you have it, so the loading sequence and the route can be planned around it. Keep your phone on, and tell the crew who to call and when. If the crew can reach you or your agent quickly, small delays stay small.

Our quotes are fixed and all inclusive, covering the van, crew, loading, transport, unloading, protection and insurance, so you know what the move costs before the day begins. If you are worried about a long wait for keys, talk it through with us before the day so the plan is agreed in advance.

What a crew can and cannot do

  • Can: load at the old property while you wait for completion, and drive to the new address ready to unload once you have the keys.
  • Cannot: unload into a home that has not completed. The keys are needed, and the crew should not ask the seller or anyone else to let them in early, or leave your belongings on the doorstep or in the garden.

If completion is on time

Loading finishes, the van heads to the new address, and the crew unloads as soon as you have the keys. Check that the crew has your number and the number of whoever is meeting them.

If completion is late in the afternoon

Loading can carry on at the old property, but the van may arrive at the new address with nowhere to unload. Ask your solicitor whether the cut-off time is likely to be an issue, and tell your removal company as early as you can. A late finish is a long day for everyone, and it is better to decide together whether to unload this evening or to take a different approach than to wait and see.

If completion does not happen today

If it becomes clear that completion will not happen today, the first call should be to your removal company, before you start making other arrangements. That way the crew can decide, with you, whether to hold the load, put it into storage or reschedule.   • Keep essentials with you. Your overnight bag, medication, chargers, important documents and valuables should stay with you, not on the van.

Think about where you will sleep tonight before you need to. If you have already left the old property, a friend's spare room or a short booking may be needed. Letting children and pets settle somewhere familiar can make a delay far easier to cope with.

A 48-hour checklist before completion

  1. Confirm the completion date and time window with your solicitor and the estate agent, and ask how you will be told when it has happened.
  2. Pack an essentials box: kettle, mugs, tea and coffee, toilet roll, basic tools, bedding for the first night and phone chargers.
  3. Take meter readings at the old property, and photograph the empty rooms once everything is out.
  4. Check access at both ends, including parking, lifts, stairs, narrow doorways and any building rules about moving times.
  5. Share all contact numbers with the crew: yours, your solicitor's, the agent's and anyone meeting the van at the other end.
  6. Sellers: arrange for keys to be left with the agent, and put away anything you do not want on the van.
  7. Charge your phone and keep a charger in your bag.

Frequently asked questions

What time do you get the keys on completion day?

There is no fixed time. Keys are released after the seller's solicitor confirms the money has arrived, which is often late morning to mid afternoon. Many contracts have a cut-off time, often around 2pm, but check the wording with your solicitor.

Who gives you the keys on completion day, the solicitor or the estate agent?

Usually the seller's estate agent, once the seller's solicitor confirms the funds. Confirm this in advance with your solicitor, as arrangements can vary.

Can the removal van load before completion has happened?

Loading at the old property can usually go ahead while you wait for your own completion. Unloading at the new property needs the keys, so the crew has to wait until completion has happened.

What happens to my belongings if completion is delayed?

That depends on the day. The options are usually holding the load, putting it into storage, or rescheduling the move. Call your removal company straight away so the crew can advise on what is possible. Storage is priced per week by volume.

Can I get my deposit back if my completion date moves?

Cancellation is free and the deposit is refunded in full. If you would rather rebook, we will agree a new date with you, subject to availability.
