Moving to Sevenoaks: The Honest Guide

Published 15 Sep 2026  |  9 min read  |  Medway & Kent Removals

Sevenoaks suits commuters who want a strong rail link, families who put schools high on the list, and buyers who want period or detached homes and accept paying a premium for them. It suits people less well if the budget is tight or if you rely on driving through the town centre at peak times. We are a removals company, not an estate agent, so this is a view from the practical side of moving. Wherever a fact can change, we point you to the official source rather than quote a figure we cannot back up.

Is Sevenoaks right for you? The short version

Sevenoaks tends to suit:

  • Commuters who value a direct rail link to London and want to live within reach of the station.
  • Families who want access to a wide choice of schools and are prepared to plan admissions carefully.
  • Buyers who like period properties in town or larger detached houses in the surrounding countryside.
  • People who want a town with shops, restaurants and green space close together.

Sevenoaks may be harder if:

  • Your budget is tight. It is generally seen as a premium location, and you should compare it with Tonbridge and Maidstone using current listings.
  • You need to drive through the centre at rush hour every day.
  • You want a large garden and a short walk to the station at the same price as somewhere further out.

None of this is a reason to rule the town in or out. It is a reason to check the details before you commit to an offer.

The commute to London

For most people the station is the main draw, and the town centre is close enough to it that many residents walk. That is a real advantage if you can live within walking distance.

Do not rely on a journey time you have read in an article, including this one. Before you make an offer, check the following yourself with the train operator or National Rail:

  • Current timetables for the services you would actually use, including early and late trains.
  • Which London terminals the fast services call at, and whether that suits your workplace.
  • Current season ticket prices for your route, and how they compare with where you live now.
  • How busy the peak services are, if you can test one in person on a weekday morning.

The part people forget is the journey to the station. If you live in a village or on the edge of town, you may drive or cycle to the station and need somewhere to leave the car. Station car parks can fill early and charge daily, so find out the cost and availability before you build it into your budget. If you are weighing this against staying in London, our guide to moving from London to Kent covers the wider trade-offs.

Schools and how to approach admissions

Sevenoaks is often chosen for its schools, and places can be competitive. If a move depends on a school place, start the process early rather than after the sale completes.

In general terms, you will be choosing between state schools, which follow local authority admissions rules, and independent schools, which set their own entry processes and charge fees. Some state schools have their own admissions criteria on top of the local authority process, and Kent has its own arrangements for secondary selection. The details change, so use the official sources instead of anything we say here:

  • Kent County Council admissions pages for application deadlines, criteria and how places are allocated.
  • Ofsted reports for each school you are considering, read in full rather than just the headline grade.
  • Each school's own admissions policy, especially for independent schools and any with specific entry tests.

Check catchment and distance rules for an address before you make an offer. Where a school allocates by distance, a house a few streets away can make a difference. Ask the school or the council how places were allocated in recent years, and do not assume that living nearby guarantees a place.

Housing and what you get for the premium

Sevenoaks generally costs more than comparable areas such as Tonbridge or Maidstone. We have not quoted house prices here because they move constantly, so compare recent sold prices on a property portal or the Land Registry for the type of home you want.

The premium buys different things depending on where you look:

  • Town centre period properties: character, walkability to the station and shops, but often smaller gardens, less parking and tighter layouts.
  • Village detached houses: more space and privacy, but usually a longer trip to the station and more reliance on the car.

Older properties bring practical points for moving day. Staircases can be narrow and turn sharply, so check whether your larger furniture will go up them. Long driveways can be soft underfoot or hard to reverse along. Some town streets have very little frontage, so the van may have to park some distance from the door. Tell us about all of this when you ask for a quote, because it affects how we plan the day. Where a home has a long carry or limited access, a photo or a short video helps a great deal.

Town or village: choosing where to live nearby

The choice between the town centre and the surrounding villages mostly comes down to three things.

  • Commute: living in or near the centre can mean a walk to the station. In a village you will usually add a drive or a bus, plus station parking.
  • Parking: town centre streets may have permit schemes and limited space. Village homes more often come with a driveway, though visitors and deliveries still need room.
  • Daily convenience: in town, shops, cafes and many schools are close. Villages offer quieter surroundings, but you may drive for a larger shop or for children's activities.

We have not named individual villages here, because a good village guide needs local knowledge and current detail. Visit at the times you would actually be there, such as the school run and the evening commute, and walk the route to the station yourself. If you are also considering other parts of West Kent, read our guide to moving to Tonbridge or Tunbridge Wells before you decide.

Moving into Sevenoaks: access, parking and timing

Moving into a busy town centre is a different job from moving into a quiet cul-de-sac. Peak-hour traffic and the one-way system can slow a large van considerably, and a missed turn costs time. An early start usually helps, because the van can get in and park before the roads fill up.

Parking is the other big factor. Check the restrictions outside your new home, including permit zones, time limits and loading rules. If space cannot be guaranteed, ask the local council about a parking suspension, and allow enough notice because these are not always available at short notice. The same applies at the address you are leaving.

We plan Sevenoaks moves around access windows, and every quote is a fixed price that includes insurance and protection for your belongings. You can read how that cover works in our explanation of goods in transit insurance. For details of how we work in the town, see our page on removals in Sevenoaks.

A short pre-move checklist:

  1. Check parking restrictions outside both addresses and apply for any suspension early.
  2. Measure the narrowest doorways, stairs and turns, and tell us about any large or awkward items.
  3. Note the distance from where the van can park to the front door.
  4. Tell us about any time limits, such as a key collection time or a lift booking.
  5. Make sure someone is reachable at each address on the day, with a phone number to share.
  6. Let neighbours know a van is coming, especially on shared or narrow roads.

Frequently asked questions

Is Sevenoaks a good place to live for commuters?

It is popular with commuters mainly because of the rail link, and the station is walkable from the town centre. Check current timetables, season ticket prices and peak services yourself, and work out how you would reach the station and where you would park.

Why is it harder to move house in Sevenoaks town centre?

Busy roads, the one-way system, parking restrictions and older properties with narrow access can all slow a move. Planning around access windows, starting early and sorting out parking in advance make a big difference.
